When Johannes Gutenberg introduced the printing press in the mid-15th century, Europe entered a new era of knowledge sharing. Books, once copied by hand and rare, became widely available. This invention fueled the Renaissance, Reformation, and Scientific Revolution, ultimately changing how societies communicated and preserved ideas.
The Library of Alexandria, built in ancient Egypt around the 3rd century BCE, was one of the most ambitious attempts to gather all human knowledge in one place. Scholars from different civilizations studied there, making it a hub of science, literature, and philosophy. Sadly, much of the library’s collection was destroyed in a series of fires, leading to one of history’s greatest intellectual losses.
