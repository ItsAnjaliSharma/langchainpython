Light travels at an incredible speed of about 299,792 kilometers per second in a vacuum. This means that sunlight takes just over eight minutes to reach Earth. The speed of light sets the ultimate speed limit for the universe, shaping theories of space, time, and relativity.

Earth’s water is constantly moving through a natural cycle. Water evaporates from oceans and rivers, forms clouds in the atmosphere, and returns as rain or snow. This cycle not only provides drinking water but also regulates climate and supports life on the planet.

Black holes are regions in space where gravity is so strong that not even light can escape. They form when massive stars collapse under their own weight. Though invisible, black holes can be detected by observing the way nearby stars and gases behave around them.
